\chapter{Bolt Pretension}
\label{chap:pretension}

Pretension in linear statics requires two coincident, planar solid faces with
separate node IDs. The preprocessor must split the mesh. FEMaster does not
modify topology. Reference-coordinate matching must be bijective: missing or
ambiguous matches are rejected.

\keywordpage{PRETENSIONSECTION}

\begin{keywordparameters}
\key{NAME} & required & Unique section name. \\
\key{SURFACE\_A} & required & First solid face set. \\
\key{SURFACE\_B} & required & Coincident, topologically separate second set. \\
\end{keywordparameters}

The section axis is the area-weighted normal of \val{SURFACE\_A}. For every
paired node, both tangential relative displacements vanish. All pairs share
one axial relative displacement in \val{FORCE} mode; this uses only physical
nodal degrees of freedom.

\keywordpage{PRETENSION}

\begin{keywordparameters}
\key{SECTION} & required & Previously defined section. \\
\key{MODE} & required & \val{FORCE} or \val{HOLD}. \\
\key{VALUE} & for FORCE & Total signed force on side B. \\
\end{keywordparameters}

The force is distributed equally across node pairs and applied in opposite
directions on the two faces. HOLD fixes the common axial gap from the preceding
successfully completed step, while subsequent loads may change the force.
Before its first action, a section has zero gap. No control node or additional
degree of freedom is created.

A failed numerical post-check rejects the step before results and step history
are accepted. It does not replace the last accepted pretension gap. HOLD without
a previously accepted gap is rejected.

Both modes use the common initial/target condition history. A new command
replaces only the target prescription for its section; the initial prescription
remains unchanged. Without a new command, the last accepted prescription is
inherited. HOLD freezes the last accepted gap when its condition is defined.
There is no separate pretension mode or load-step manager on the section.

\begin{femasterdeck}
*PRETENSIONSECTION, NAME=PT1, SURFACE_A=CUT_A, SURFACE_B=CUT_B
*LOADCASE, TYPE=LINEARSTATIC, NAME=PRELOAD
*PRETENSION, SECTION=PT1, MODE=FORCE, VALUE=1000
*CONSTRAINTMETHOD, TYPE=NULLSPACE
*END
*LOADCASE, TYPE=LINEARSTATIC, NAME=SERVICE
*PRETENSION, SECTION=PT1, MODE=HOLD
*CONSTRAINTMETHOD, TYPE=NULLSPACE
*END
\end{femasterdeck}

Use NULLSPACE or ELIMINATION. LAGRANGE, nonlinear and perturbation steps are
unsupported for pretension.

\section{Results}

\val{PTGAP} gives the axial relative displacement on both nodes of each pair.
\val{PTFORC} gives opposite transmitted force vectors on the two faces.
The signed section force is the sum of \val{PTFORC} on side B projected onto
the section axis. \val{TF} repeats that total on side B nodes. Additional active supports, couplings, MPCs, ties, connectors, rigid-body constraints
and manual equations on either cut face are rejected. Place constraints away from the cut;
external loads at cut nodes remain supported.

\section{Validation scope}

The focused regression suite covers FORCE followed by HOLD and external loading,
oblique cut axes, both supported constraint methods and two independent sections
with different axes and forces. It checks common axial gap, zero tangential
relative displacement, opposite interface forces and analytical section-force
values. It also checks rejection of invalid node pairing, additional constraints
at the cut and failed numerical post-checks.

For a fixed-ended straight bar with $E=210000$, $\nu=0$, $A=4$ and length $L=3$,
a pretension force of $1000$ produces a gap of $1000L/(EA)=0.00357142857$.
A total axial load of $-4$ at length coordinate $2$ increases the compressive
force at the held cut at coordinate $1$ to $1000+4/3$.
These are internal analytical regressions; comparison with an external solver
has not been completed. They do not establish that the full repository test
suite passes.
